# Defer Judgment, Go for Quantity

During divergent phases, withhold all evaluation — no scoring, no ranking, no
"yes, but." Volume precedes quality; the pool must be large before selection is meaningful.

- Osborn (1953) and Linus Pauling: "the best way to have a good idea is to have lots of ideas";
  IDEO targets ~100 ideas in 60 minutes of ideation
- Evaluation apprehension — the fear of social judgment — is one of the three primary suppressors
  of group ideation (Diehl & Stroebe, 1987, 1991); withholding evaluation removes this suppressor
- Amabile's Intrinsic Motivation Principle (1983, 1996): external evaluation during generation
  shifts motivation from intrinsic to extrinsic and measurably suppresses creative output
- Divergent thinking metrics — fluency (quantity), flexibility (category variety), originality
  (novelty), elaboration (detail) — are all maximized *before* convergent selection begins
- Concretely: set a count target before evaluating ("let's get to 15 ideas before we talk about
  which ones are best")
