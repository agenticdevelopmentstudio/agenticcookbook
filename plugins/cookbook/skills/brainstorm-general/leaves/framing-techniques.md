# Framing Techniques

Concrete techniques for surfacing and reframing the real problem before idea generation begins.
MUST be applied before any divergent phase; premature ideation on a misframed problem wastes all
generative effort.

## How Might We (HMW)

Replace closed problem statements with generative invitations. Syntax: "How **Might** We [verb]
[goal]?" The word "might" is critical — it invites speculation without commitment.

- **hmw-reframe-first**: Facilitator MUST generate at least one HMW framing of the seed idea
  before any ideation begins.
- **hmw-multiple-angles**: Facilitator SHOULD generate 2–4 HMW variants reframing the problem
  from different angles — broadened, narrowed, user-reversed, and domain-shifted — to surface the
  richest generative direction.
- **hmw-syntax**: The question MUST use the exact three-word opener "How Might We" (not "How
  could we" or "How should we") to preserve the speculative, shared framing.

Source: IDEO (originator); Min Basadur (1990s); Knapp, Zeratsky & Kowitz, *Sprint*, 2016.

## Five Whys

Iterative root-cause technique: ask "Why?" five times in sequence to drill past stated symptoms
to root causes.

- **five-whys-before-generating**: Facilitator SHOULD apply Five Whys when the seed sounds like
  a solution ("I want to build X") rather than a goal, to surface the underlying need.
- **five-whys-chain**: Each "Why?" MUST target the answer to the prior "Why?", not the original
  symptom statement.
- **five-whys-stop**: Five Whys SHOULD stop when the answer is a root cause (an unchangeable
  constraint or a genuine underlying need), not after exactly five iterations.

Source: Toyoda, Sakichi (~1930s); Ohno, T., *Toyota Production System*, 1988.

## First-Principles Thinking

Strip inherited assumptions back to fundamental truths; rebuild the solution space from those
facts alone rather than by analogy to existing solutions.

- **first-principles-list-assumptions**: Facilitator MUST invite the user to name assumptions
  explicitly ("What are you taking for granted here?") before applying first-principles reasoning.
- **first-principles-rebuild**: After surfacing assumptions, facilitator SHOULD invite the user to
  reconstruct the goal as if none of the identified assumptions were true.
- **first-principles-distinguish**: Facilitator MUST distinguish first-principles thinking
  ("what is fundamentally true?") from analogical reasoning ("how did others solve this?") — they
  serve different purposes and MUST NOT be conflated.

Source: Aristotle (philosophical roots); Paul & Elder, *Critical Thinking*, 2001; associated with
Elon Musk's SpaceX/Tesla applications (interviews, 2012–2013).
