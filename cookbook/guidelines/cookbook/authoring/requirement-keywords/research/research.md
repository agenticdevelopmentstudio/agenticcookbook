# Research: requirement-keywords

The keyword definitions hold. RFC 2119 makes MUST and MUST NOT absolute. SHOULD and SHOULD NOT are recommendations: before deviating from one, its implications must be weighed. MAY is truly optional. ISO/IEC Annex H agrees in its shall, should and may forms.

Three rules are stronger than the sources:

- No standard requires keywords for every requirement. RFC 8174 says normative text does not require them. So "MUST use RFC 2119 keywords" is supported only at SHOULD; it appears in gf-rfc-keywords, in conventions.md and in the artifact-formatting rule. It needs a companion MUST that says how text without a keyword is read (W3C QA Framework Requirement 7).
- The "bold or uppercase" rule is contradicted by its bold alternative. Under RFC 8174 the words carry their BCP 14 meaning only in all capitals.

The severity rules hold. In Vale, ESLint, markdownlint, Bikeshed and GitHub code scanning, only error-level findings fail the run or the merge check; warnings do not. That supports approve-on-zero-fail. It also supports a mapping of MUST to an error, SHOULD to a warning and MAY to no finding, though that mapping is an inference: no source states it.

Twelve practices are missing from the topic and each gets a `new` verdict:

- uppercase-only meaning
- flagging lowercase keywords
- stating the interpretation
- stating how text without a keyword is read
- no keywords in non-normative text
- the severity mapping
- warnings that do not block
- one keyword per requirement
- the core keyword subset
- no "MUST not" or "MAY NOT"
- one keyword set, never mixed
- keywords only where they are needed

## Best practices

*Best practices: what is current best practice for this topic?*

- **rfc2119-must-absolute-requirement**: MUST (and its synonyms REQUIRED and SHALL) marks an absolute requirement of the specification. Quote: "MUST This word, or the terms "REQUIRED" or "SHALL", mean that the definition is an absolute requirement of the specification." Source: [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119.txt).
- **rfc2119-must-not-absolute-prohibition**: MUST NOT (and SHALL NOT) marks an absolute prohibition. Quote: "MUST NOT This phrase, or the phrase "SHALL NOT", mean that the definition is an absolute prohibition of the specification." Source: [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119.txt).
- **rfc2119-should-recommendation**: SHOULD (RECOMMENDED) is a recommendation. Valid reasons to ignore it may exist, but its full implications must be understood and carefully weighed before deviating. Quote: "SHOULD This word, or the adjective "RECOMMENDED", mean that there may exist valid reasons in particular circumstances to ignore a particular item, but the full implications must be understood and carefully weighed before choosing a different course." Source: [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119.txt).
- **rfc2119-should-not-recommendation**: SHOULD NOT (NOT RECOMMENDED) is the negative recommendation. Quote: "SHOULD NOT This phrase, or the phrase "NOT RECOMMENDED" mean that there may exist valid reasons in particular circumstances when the particular behavior is acceptable or even useful, but the full implications should be understood and the case carefully weighed before implementing any behavior described with this label." Source: [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119.txt).
- **rfc2119-may-truly-optional**: MAY (OPTIONAL) marks a truly optional item. Quote: "MAY This word, or the adjective "OPTIONAL", mean that an item is truly optional." Source: [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119.txt).
- **keywords-used-sparingly**: The keywords are to be used with care and sparingly, only where they are actually required. Quote: "Imperatives of the type defined in this memo must be used with care and sparingly. In particular, they MUST only be used where it is actually required for interoperation or to limit behavior which has potential for causing harm". Source: [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119.txt).
- **keywords-not-required-for-normative-text**: Using the keywords is not required, and normative text does not need them. Quote: "These words can be used as defined here, but using them is not required. Specifically, normative text does not require the use of these key words. They are used for clarity and consistency when that is what's wanted, but a lot of normative text does not use them and is still normative." Source: [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174.txt).
- **uppercase-only-meaning**: Only the UPPERCASE forms carry the BCP 14 meanings. Quote: "This document updates RFC 2119 by clarifying that only UPPERCASE usage of the key words have the defined special meanings." Source: [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174.txt).
- **lowercase-has-normal-english-meaning**: Lowercase forms have their normal English meanings. Quote: "When these words are not capitalized, they have their normal English meanings and are not affected by this document." Source: [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174.txt).
- **bcp14-boilerplate**: A document that follows BCP 14 states a boilerplate near its beginning, saying the keywords carry their meaning only in all capitals. Quote: "The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here." Source: [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174.txt).
- **state-the-interpretation**: A document must cite RFC 2119 normatively or state its own interpretation of the capitalized words. Quote: "RFC 2119 [BCP14] defines a default interpretation of these capitalized words in IETF documents. If this interpretation is used, RFC 2119 must be cited (as specified in RFC 2119) and included as a normative reference. Otherwise, the correct interpretation must be specified in the document." Source: [RFC 7322, RFC Style Guide](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7322.txt).
- **qa-consistent-style-explain**: Requirements come in several styles: keywords, the imperative voice and descriptive assertions. Tell readers which styles a specification uses. Quote: "Specifications use different styles to convey conformance requirements: RFC 2119 [RFC2119] keywords, imperative voice, descriptive assertions, etc. Tell the readers what styles are used, especially when the specification uses different styles for different parts of the specification." Source: [QA Framework: Specification Guidelines](https://www.w3.org/TR/qaframe-spec/).
- **keywords-make-requirements-easy-to-spot**: Keywords make requirements easy to spot, and they are usually given distinctive formatting. Quote: "Using RFC 2119 [RFC2119] key words (MUST, SHOULD, MAY, …) makes it easy to spot conformance requirements. ... They are usually written with distinctive formatting, such as uppercase or bold." Source: [QA Framework: Specification Guidelines](https://www.w3.org/TR/qaframe-spec/).
- **qa-indicate-levels**: A specification must indicate which requirements are mandatory, which recommended and which optional. Quote: "Indicate which conformance requirements are mandatory, which are recommended, and which are optional." Source: [QA Framework: Specification Guidelines](https://www.w3.org/TR/qaframe-spec/).
- **qa-conformance-classes-by-level**: Requirement levels can define conformance classes, with MUST and SHOULD judged separately. Quote: "For instance, HTTP 1.1 [HTTP11] defines two types of conformance, one where all the MUST are respected, and one where all the MUST and the SHOULD are implemented." Source: [QA Framework: Specification Guidelines](https://www.w3.org/TR/qaframe-spec/).
- **qa-subject-keyword-operation**: A good keyword requirement takes the form subject, one keyword, operation. Quote: "A good conformance requirement using RFC Keyword is of the form: subject rfc_keyword operation, where subject is one of the classes of products, rfc_keyword one of MUST, SHOULD, MAY, and operation a verb describing one of the operations the classes of products can do". Source: [QA Framework: Specification Guidelines](https://www.w3.org/TR/qaframe-spec/).
- **qa-no-normative-language-in-informative**: Avoid language that looks normative in informative sections. Quote: "Avoid using language that looks normative (e.g., with RFC 2119 key words) in informative sections." Source: [QA Framework: Specification Guidelines](https://www.w3.org/TR/qaframe-spec/).

### The rules under verification

- **gf-rfc-keywords-prior** (`gf-rfc-keywords`, unsupported): "Requirements within the guideline MUST use RFC 2119 keywords (MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, MAY) to indicate obligation levels." No source requires keywords. RFC 8174 says normative text does not require them (keywords-not-required-for-normative-text). The QA Framework treats keywords as one style among several (qa-consistent-style-explain). OASIS says only "should use" (oasis-should-use-rfc2119). **The sources support SHOULD**: use the keywords for requirements, with a MUST to declare how other text is read (see state-how-keywordless-text-is-read).
- **gf-rfc-keywords-bold-or-uppercase-prior** (`gf-rfc-keywords-bold-or-uppercase`, contradicted): "Keywords MUST be bold or uppercase when used normatively." Bold is contradicted. Under BCP 14 the meaning attaches only to all capitals (uppercase-only-meaning, bcp14-boilerplate), and lowercase words keep their normal English meaning whatever their styling (lowercase-has-normal-english-meaning). OASIS agrees (oasis-uppercase-only). The uppercase alternative alone is supported, at MUST, as keywords-meaningful-only-in-uppercase.
- **use-rfc-2119-keywords-for-behavioral-requirements-prior** (`use-rfc-2119-keywords-for-behavioral-requirements`, unsupported; a finding against `guide/introduction/conventions.md`): "Use RFC 2119 keywords for all behavioral requirements:" The imperative reads at MUST strength, but no source makes keywords obligatory for every requirement. Normative text without a keyword is still normative (keywords-not-required-for-normative-text, tab-keywordless-must-equivalent). **The sources support SHOULD.** conventions.md is not changed in this stage; this note records the finding.
- **must-is-absolute-requirement** (supported; conventions.md): RFC 2119 defines MUST as an absolute requirement and MUST NOT as an absolute prohibition (rfc2119-must-absolute-requirement, rfc2119-must-not-absolute-prohibition). ISO/IEC shall agrees (iso-shall-requirement).
- **should-is-recommendation** (supported; conventions.md): rfc2119-should-recommendation, rfc2119-should-not-recommendation and iso-should-preferred all support it. RFC 2119's definition adds a condition that the one-word definition leaves out: the implications must be weighed before deviating.
- **may-is-optional-behavior** (supported; conventions.md): rfc2119-may-truly-optional and iso-may-permission support it.
- **rule-uses-rfc-2119-keywords-prior** (`rule-uses-rfc-2119-keywords`, unsupported): "**Use RFC 2119 keywords** for requirements — MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, MAY." As with gf-rfc-keywords, **the sources support SHOULD** (keywords-not-required-for-normative-text, oasis-should-use-rfc2119). Infra's encouragement to limit the set to the core words supports the five-keyword list only as a SHOULD (infra-core-subset).
- **follow-must-address-should-checks** (supported): MUST is absolute and SHOULD can be set aside for valid reasons (rfc2119-must-absolute-requirement, rfc2119-should-recommendation). The QA Framework shows the two levels judged separately (qa-indicate-levels, qa-conformance-classes-by-level). "Documented reason" is stricter than RFC 2119, which asks only that the implications be "understood and carefully weighed". That gap is left as an open question.

## Known solutions

*Known solutions: what solutions, patterns, libraries and tools already exist, and how do they compare?*

- **respec-conformance-boilerplate**: ReSpec inserts the RFC 2119 boilerplate for a conformance section. Quote: "A <section> with id="conformance" tells ReSpec to insert the standard RFC 2119 conformance boilerplate." Source: [ReSpec Documentation](https://respec.org/docs/).
- **respec-warns-missing-conformance**: ReSpec warns when keywords appear without the conformance section. Quote: "ReSpec will warn you if RFC 2119 keywords are present but the conformance section is missing:" Source: [ReSpec Documentation](https://respec.org/docs/).
- **respec-lint-severity**: ReSpec sets each lint rule to error, warning or disabled. Quote: "Rule severity can be true (error), "warn" (warning), or false (disabled)". Source: [ReSpec Documentation](https://respec.org/docs/).
- **bikeshed-error-levels**: Bikeshed grades its messages fatal, link-error, warning and lint. Quote: "clear mistakes are "fatal", errors in resolving autolinks are "link-error", things that look a little questionable but are totally okay to generate a document with are "warning", and things that just violate one of the opt-in lint checks are "lint"." Source: [Bikeshed Documentation](https://speced.github.io/bikeshed/).
- **bikeshed-die-on**: Bikeshed's `--die-on` flag sets which levels make it fail with a non-zero exit. Quote: "The --die-on flag controls which sorts of errors cause Bikeshed to fail to produce output and exit with a non-0 status". Source: [Bikeshed Documentation](https://speced.github.io/bikeshed/).
- **bikeshed-die-on-default-fatal**: By default only fatal messages stop Bikeshed. Quote: "Defaults to "fatal"." Source: [Bikeshed Documentation](https://speced.github.io/bikeshed/).
- **bikeshed-accidental-2119**: Bikeshed has an opt-in lint for keywords in non-normative sections. Quote: "accidental-2119 (default: off) Complains about usage of RFC 2119 terms (may, must, should, etc.) in non-normative sections." Source: [Bikeshed Documentation](https://speced.github.io/bikeshed/).
- **bikeshed-lowercase-for-readability**: Bikeshed's own conformance text declares a lowercase convention. Quote: "However, for readability, these words do not appear in all uppercase letters in this specification." Source: [Bikeshed Documentation](https://speced.github.io/bikeshed/).
- **idnits-mixed-case-not**: IETF idnits flags "MUST not" and similar mixed-case negations. Quote: "Using lowercase 'not' together with uppercase 'MUST', 'SHALL', 'SHOULD', or 'RECOMMENDED' is not an accepted usage according to RFC 2119. Please use uppercase 'NOT' together with RFC 2119 keywords (if that is what you mean)." Source: [idnits source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ietf-tools/idnits/main/idnits).
- **idnits-may-not**: idnits flags "MAY NOT". Quote: "The expression 'MAY NOT', while looking like RFC 2119 requirements text, is not defined in RFC 2119, and should not be used. Consider using 'MUST NOT' instead (if that is what you mean)." Source: [idnits source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ietf-tools/idnits/main/idnits).
- **idnits-boilerplate-check**: idnits checks for the RFC 2119 reference and boilerplate. A missing reference is an error, and a boilerplate problem alone is a warning. Quote: "both a reference to RFC 2119 and the recommended RFC 2119 boilerplate, even if it appears to use RFC 2119 keywords". Source: [idnits source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ietf-tools/idnits/main/idnits); [idnits (Author Tools)](https://author-tools.ietf.org/idnits).
- **vale-only-errors-nonzero**: Vale has three levels, and only errors set a non-zero exit code. Quote: "suggestion, warning, or error. Only errors set a non-zero exit code." Source: [Vale: Styles](https://vale.sh/docs/styles).
- **vale-minalertlevel-ci**: Vale can show every alert in the editor while running only error-level alerts in CI. Quote: "This allows you to, for example, show all alerts in your editor while only running error-level alerts in CI." Source: [Vale: MinAlertLevel](https://vale.sh/docs/keys/minalertlevel).
- **eslint-warn-no-exit-code**: In ESLint, warn reports without affecting the exit code, and error sets the exit code to 1. Quote: ""warn" or 1 - turn the rule on as a warning (doesn’t affect exit code)." Source: [ESLint: Configure Rules](https://eslint.org/docs/latest/use/configure/rules).
- **eslint-error-for-ci**: ESLint rules are set to error so that CI, pre-commit checks and merges are enforced. Quote: "Rules are typically set to "error" to enforce compliance with the rule during continuous integration testing, pre-commit checks, and pull request merging because doing so causes ESLint to exit with a non-zero exit code." Source: [ESLint: Configure Rules](https://eslint.org/docs/latest/use/configure/rules).
- **eslint-warn-for-uncertain**: ESLint uses warn for a rule that may give false positives and needs manual review. Quote: "or when a rule cannot determine with certainty that a problem has been found (when a rule might have false positives and need manual review)". Source: [ESLint: Configure Rules](https://eslint.org/docs/latest/use/configure/rules).
- **eslint-max-warnings**: Warnings alone pass, unless an optional `--max-warnings` threshold is exceeded. Quote: "Normally, if ESLint runs and finds no errors (only warnings), it exits with a success exit status. However, if --max-warnings is specified and the total warning count is greater than the specified threshold, ESLint exits with an error status." Source: [ESLint: Command Line Interface Reference](https://eslint.org/docs/latest/use/command-line-interface).
- **markdownlint-error-or-warning**: markdownlint reports each rule's violations as errors or as warnings. Quote: "The values true or "error" enable a rule in its default configuration and report violations as errors. The value "warning" enables a rule in its default configuration and reports violations as warnings." Source: [markdownlint](https://github.com/DavidAnson/markdownlint).
- **markdownlint-cli2-exit-codes**: markdownlint-cli2 exits 0 when there are no errors, even if there are warnings. Quote: "0: Linting was successful and there were no errors (there may be warnings)". Source: [markdownlint-cli2](https://github.com/DavidAnson/markdownlint-cli2).
- **sarif-error-level**: In SARIF, error means a serious problem. Quote: ""error": The rule specified by ruleId was evaluated and a serious problem was found." Source: [SARIF v2.1.0 Plus Errata 01](https://docs.oasis-open.org/sarif/sarif/v2.1.0/errata01/os/sarif-v2.1.0-errata01-os-complete.html).
- **sarif-warning-level**: In SARIF, warning means a problem. Quote: ""warning": The rule specified by ruleId was evaluated and a problem was found." Source: [SARIF v2.1.0 Plus Errata 01](https://docs.oasis-open.org/sarif/sarif/v2.1.0/errata01/os/sarif-v2.1.0-errata01-os-complete.html).
- **sarif-note-level**: In SARIF, note means a minor problem or an opportunity to improve. Quote: ""note": The rule specified by ruleId was evaluated and a minor problem or an opportunity to improve the code was found." Source: [SARIF v2.1.0 Plus Errata 01](https://docs.oasis-open.org/sarif/sarif/v2.1.0/errata01/os/sarif-v2.1.0-errata01-os-complete.html).
- **github-check-fails-on-error**: GitHub's code scanning check fails on error severity and passes when there are only warnings or notes. Quote: "If the code scanning results check finds any problems with a severity of error, critical, or high, the check fails and the error is reported in the check results. If all the results found by code scanning have lower severities, the alerts are treated as warnings or notes and the check succeeds." Source: [Triaging code scanning alerts in pull requests](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/code-scanning/managing-code-scanning-alerts/triaging-code-scanning-alerts-in-pull-requests).
- **github-ruleset-blocks-merge**: In GitHub, the severity that blocks a merge is set in the ruleset. Quote: "A required tool finds a code scanning alert of a severity that is defined in the ruleset." Source: [Editing your configuration of default setup](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/code-scanning/managing-your-code-scanning-configuration/editing-your-configuration-of-default-setup).

### How they compare

Every tool here keeps the same split between levels:

- One level fails the run, the build or the merge check: error in Vale, ESLint and markdownlint, fatal in Bikeshed by default, and error/critical/high in GitHub code scanning.
- One or more lower levels report without blocking: warning and suggestion (Vale), warn (ESLint), warning (markdownlint, Bikeshed, ReSpec), and note (SARIF).

A stricter gate is always opt-in: ESLint's `--max-warnings`, Bikeshed's `--die-on`, Vale's `MinAlertLevel`, and GitHub ruleset severities.

The spec checkers (idnits, ReSpec, Bikeshed) add keyword-specific checks:

- the boilerplate or conformance section is present;
- no "MUST not" or "MAY NOT";
- no keywords in non-normative sections.

ReSpec raises its missing-conformance check as a warning. Bikeshed's accidental-2119 is an opt-in lint.

This supports **approve-on-zero-fail** (supported): approval blocks only on the failing level (vale-only-errors-nonzero, eslint-error-for-ci, markdownlint-cli2-exit-codes, github-check-fails-on-error, bikeshed-die-on, bikeshed-die-on-default-fatal).

No source states a mapping from requirement keyword to severity. **keyword-sets-finding-severity** (new, SHOULD) is an inference from the sources, mapping each keyword to a lint level:

| Keyword | Meaning (source) | Lint level |
|---|---|---|
| MUST / MUST NOT | absolute (RFC 2119) | error, which in the cookbook's lint is FAIL |
| SHOULD / SHOULD NOT | deviation allowed after weighing (RFC 2119) | warning, which is WARN |
| MAY | truly optional (RFC 2119) | no finding, or at most a note |

The levels themselves come from SARIF: error is a serious problem, warning a problem, and note a minor problem. The split also matches the HTTP/1.1 conformance classes and ESLint's use of warn where a rule needs human review.

**warn-findings-do-not-block** (new, SHOULD) records that warnings do not block by default; a warning threshold is opt-in.

On these mappings, the lint-artifact GF-03 level example, "WARN if missing", classifies as a SHOULD-level check.

## Platform guidelines

*Platform guidelines: what do the platform owners' own guidelines say, for each platform the topic touches (Apple, Android, Windows, web, the databases and runtimes concerned)?*

For this topic the platforms are the standards bodies (IETF, W3C, WHATWG, OASIS, ISO/IEC, ETSI). The IETF's own rules are under Best practices (RFC 2119, RFC 8174, RFC 7322) and the tools under Known solutions.

- **w3c-mos-uppercase-em**: The W3C Manual of Style advises that keywords used in the RFC sense be uppercase, wrapped in `em` and given the `rfc2119` CSS class. Quote: "When these key words are used in the RFC sense, make them UPPERCASE, enclose them in the em element, and style them with CSS class of rfc2119 to make the UPPERCASE readable." Source: [W3C Manual of Style](https://www.w3.org/guide/manual-of-style/).
- **w3c-mos-no-keywords-informative**: The Manual of Style advises marking informative sections and using no keywords in them. Quote: "If some sections are informative, say so at the start of each section, and do not use RFC 2119 keywords in those sections". Source: [W3C Manual of Style](https://www.w3.org/guide/manual-of-style/).
- **infra-lowercase-equivalent**: WHATWG Infra gives the lowercase keywords the same meaning, and bars them from non-normative content. Quote: "These keywords have equivalent meaning when written in lowercase and cannot appear in non-normative content." Source: [Infra Standard](https://infra.spec.whatwg.org/).
- **infra-willful-violation**: Infra's lowercase convention is a declared willful violation of RFC 8174. Quote: "This is a willful violation of RFC 8174, motivated by legibility and a desire to preserve long-standing practice in many non-IETF-published pre-RFC 8174 documents." Source: [Infra Standard](https://infra.spec.whatwg.org/).
- **infra-core-subset**: Infra encourages limiting the keywords to must, must not, should and may. Quote: "Documents using this standard are encouraged to limit themselves to "must", "must not", "should", and "may", and to use these in their lowercase form as that is generally considered to be more readable." Source: [Infra Standard](https://infra.spec.whatwg.org/).
- **infra-no-keywords-in-non-normative**: Infra declares which content is non-normative and treats everything else as normative. Quote: "All assertions, diagrams, examples, and notes are non-normative, as are all sections explicitly marked non-normative. Everything else is normative." Source: [Infra Standard](https://infra.spec.whatwg.org/).
- **oasis-uppercase-only**: OASIS writes the keywords in uppercase; in lowercase they state no requirement. Quote: "[RFC 2119] keywords are written in UPPERCASE. When [RFC2119] keywords are written in lowercase, they have only their normal English usage meaning. In lowercase, [RFC2119] keywords do not state normative or conformance requirements." Source: [Keyword Guidelines for OASIS Specifications and Standards](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=3585).
- **oasis-case-change-is-material**: Under OASIS, changing a keyword's case is a material change. Quote: "WARNING: Changing an [RFC 2119] keyword, such as lowercase “must” to UPPERCASE “MUST”, changes the conformance requirements of a specification. If that happens, it is a material change that requires a public review." Source: [Keyword Guidelines for OASIS Specifications and Standards](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=3585).
- **oasis-should-use-rfc2119**: OASIS work products should use RFC 2119 keywords; this is a recommendation. Quote: "All TC work products that will become OASIS TC specifications or OASIS standards, should use [RFC 2119] keywords." Source: [Keyword Guidelines for OASIS Specifications and Standards](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=3585).
- **oasis-never-mix-keyword-sets**: OASIS uses either the RFC 2119 set or the ISO/IEC set, never both. Quote: "Every OASIS specification or standard will choose (and use) one or the other. The two keyword sets are never mixed in a specification or standard." Source: [Keyword Guidelines for OASIS Specifications and Standards](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=3585).
- **oasis-may-not-not-keyword**: OASIS does not treat "MAY NOT" as a keyword. Quote: "Is “MAY NOT” a keyword? No, although there is an example of “MAY NOT” in an OASIS specification." Source: [Keyword Guidelines for OASIS Specifications and Standards](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=3585).
- **oasis-iso-keywords-by-definition**: ISO/IEC keywords are keywords by definition, not by typography. Quote: "In the ISO/IEC examples note that keywords do not require UPPER case or bolding in order to be keywords. They are keywords by definition, not typography." Source: [Keyword Guidelines for OASIS Specifications and Standards](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=3585).
- **iso-shall-requirement**: In ISO/IEC, shall marks a requirement from which no deviation is permitted. Quote: "The verbal forms shown in Table H.1 shall be used to indicate requirements strictly to be followed in order to conform to the document and from which no deviation is permitted." Source: [ISO/IEC Directives Part 2 (2004), Annex H](https://www.mhonarc.org/archive/html/ietf/2011-09/pdfBUKoWRBJxm.pdf).
- **iso-should-preferred**: In ISO/IEC, should marks a preferred course that is not required. Quote: "that a certain course of action is preferred but not necessarily required, or that (in the negative form) a certain possibility or course of action is deprecated but not prohibited." Source: [ISO/IEC Directives Part 2 (2004), Annex H](https://www.mhonarc.org/archive/html/ietf/2011-09/pdfBUKoWRBJxm.pdf).
- **iso-may-permission**: In ISO/IEC, may marks a permitted course of action. Quote: "The verbal forms shown in Table H.3 shall be used to indicate a course of action permissible within the limits of the document." Source: [ISO/IEC Directives Part 2 (2004), Annex H](https://www.mhonarc.org/archive/html/ietf/2011-09/pdfBUKoWRBJxm.pdf).
- **iso-no-must-for-shall**: ISO/IEC forbids must as an alternative to shall. Quote: "Do not use “must” as an alternative for “shall”. (This will avoid any confusion between the requirements of a document and external statutory obligations.)" Source: [ISO/IEC Directives Part 2 (2004), Annex H](https://www.mhonarc.org/archive/html/ietf/2011-09/pdfBUKoWRBJxm.pdf).
- **iso-no-may-not**: ISO/IEC forbids may not as a prohibition. Quote: "Do not use “may not” instead of “shall not” to express a prohibition." Source: [ISO/IEC Directives Part 2 (2004), Annex H](https://www.mhonarc.org/archive/html/ietf/2011-09/pdfBUKoWRBJxm.pdf).
- **etsi-requirements-only-normative**: ETSI states requirements only in normative clauses. Quote: "The requirements shall be mentioned only in normative clauses and deliverables". Source: [What about requirements in ETSI deliverables?](https://portal.etsi.org/Services/editHelp/Search/FAQs/Requirements).

The case question splits the platforms. RFC 8174 and OASIS give keywords their meaning only in uppercase. WHATWG Infra, Bikeshed's own text and ISO/IEC read lowercase keywords as keywords, by declared definition. Both camps make the convention explicit in the document. The cookbook must pick one, which is an open question. Whichever it picks, **keywords-meaningful-only-in-uppercase** (new) is supported at MUST for a cookbook that invokes BCP 14.

## The expert's view

*The expert's view: if we hired an expert consultant in this topic, what would they tell us: what to do, what to avoid, and what people usually get wrong?*

- **tab-drop-formatting-requirement**: A W3C/WHATWG spec editor recommends dropping the requirement to format keywords specially. Quote: "So I strongly recommend that we amend the Manual Of Style to drop the formatting requirement." Source: [Tab Atkins Jr., Re: RFC2119 Styling](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/spec-prod/2023AprJun/0017.html).
- **tab-keywordless-must-equivalent**: Many requirements carry no keyword: "is" statements and algorithm steps are MUST-equivalent. Quote: "they'll be missing a ton anyway where we say something "is" X, or "To X: <list steps>", which are also MUST-equivalent." Source: [Tab Atkins Jr., Re: RFC2119 Styling](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/spec-prod/2023AprJun/0017.html).
- **fantasai-styling-requirement**: The Manual of Style's styling convention is uppercase inside `em` with extra styling. Quote: "which requires RFC2119 keywords to be encoded in uppercase inside an <em> element with some extra styling". Source: [fantasai, RFC2119 Styling](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/spec-prod/2023AprJun/0016.html).
- **sperberg-mcqueen-lowercase-misuse**: What people get wrong: some "must" or "may" in plain English always slips in. Quote: "I do not remember ever seeing such claims confirmed by inspection: there was *always" some use of "must" which meant "it will in the nature of things always be the case that ...", or some use of "may" meaning "it could happen that ..."". Source: [C. M. Sperberg-McQueen, Re: RFC2119 Styling](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/spec-prod/2023AprJun/0018.html).
- **sperberg-mcqueen-focus-attention**: Marking keywords focuses attention on what is a requirement. Quote: "it does help focus WG attention on the question of what is and what is not a requirement for conformance." Source: [C. M. Sperberg-McQueen, Re: RFC2119 Styling](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/spec-prod/2023AprJun/0018.html).
- **sperberg-mcqueen-iso-avoids-nonstandard-senses**: ISO/IEC manages without uppercase because it never uses the verbs in other senses. Quote: "ISO/IEC standards appear to get by just fine without uppercasing the verbs relevant to conformance -- partly because they do go to great lengths to ensure that the relevant verbs are not used in non-standard senses." Source: [C. M. Sperberg-McQueen, Re: RFC2119 Styling](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/spec-prod/2023AprJun/0018.html).
- **fantasai-lowercase-misuse-is-editorial-error**: Spec groups treat a keyword used in its ordinary sense as an editorial error. Quote: "Afaict, the CSSWG, WHATWG, and at least some other groups consider such instances to be editorial errors, and will make an effort to reword these occurrences." Source: [fantasai, Re: RFC2119 Styling](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/spec-prod/2023AprJun/0019.html).
- **caceres-respec-defaults-bcp14**: ReSpec defaults to BCP 14 and already warns about keywords in non-normative blocks. Quote: "Right now, Respec defaults to BCP14. IIRC, Respec already warns about incorrect RFC2119 in non-normative blocks (notes, examples, etc.), but could make it more strict." Source: [Marcos Caceres, Re: RFC2119 Styling](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/spec-prod/2023AprJun/0024.html).
- **lint-unmarked-keyword-usage**: One proposal is to mark keyword uses explicitly and lint every unmarked use. Quote: "introduce a very minimal shorthand for explicitly stating that you mean the 2119 keyword, and then lint for any usages that aren't marked up as such". Source: [Marcos Caceres quoting Tab Atkins Jr., Re: RFC2119 Styling](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/spec-prod/2023AprJun/0024.html).
- **w3c-wiki-active-form**: Write "X MUST do Y", not "X MUST be Y". Quote: "one should not say "X MUST be Y" (grammatical form: passive), but "X MUST do Y"". Source: [W3C Wiki: RfcKeywords](https://www.w3.org/wiki/RfcKeywords).
- **plainlanguage-must-not-shall**: Use one word for requirements, and never mix must with shall. Quote: "Use "must" not "shall" to impose requirements." Source: [plainlanguage.gov: Shall and must](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GSA/plainlanguage.gov/main/_pages/guidelines/conversational/shall-and-must.md).
