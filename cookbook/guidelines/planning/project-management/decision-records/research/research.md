# Research: decision-records

Topic `decision-records`, area `planning/project-management`. Researched 2026-11-24 at base commit f0dd450e1188364e9e221d912d49657944ae46ad. Every finding names the claim in `research.json` that holds its quote and sources.

Apple, Android and Windows publish no guidance on decision records; the topic has no bearing on those platforms.

## Best practices

- **Context, decision and consequences are the minimum.** AWS names the three as the least an ADR holds. Nygard's original record opens with the context, the forces at play. The GOV.UK template lists context, decision and consequences among its fields (`record-context-of-decision`). Every record states the reason as well as the decision. Microsoft says a record without justification loses its value, and Fowler and AWS agree (`record-rationale-justification`). Consequences include the costs: Nygard asks for all of them, "not just the positive ones", and Microsoft warns against hiding them (`record-consequences-good-and-bad`). **record-decisions-with-full-context** is supported at SHOULD. The owners write "should" and "always", and the sources would bear MUST for context and rationale.
- **Alternatives considered.** Microsoft's log documents "alternatives that you ruled out". Google Cloud's ADR captures "the key options available". Fowler calls listing the serious alternatives with their pros and cons valuable (`record-alternatives-considered`). Rust RFCs and PEPs require a section for alternatives and rejected ideas (`proposal-processes-record-alternatives`). Nygard's original template and the GOV.UK template have no alternatives section. **alternatives-are-recorded** is supported at SHOULD.
- **Who decided.** MADR carries a `decision-makers` field, PEPs record the PEP-Delegate, and DACI names the Approver (`record-who-decided`). MADR marks that metadata optional and Nygard's template has no decider. **capture-rationale-alternatives-and-decider** is supported at SHOULD: rationale at SHOULD-to-MUST, and alternatives and the decider at SHOULD. **decision-has-rationale-and-decider** is supported at SHOULD for the decider and could be MUST for the rationale. Whether the decider becomes a MUST is an open question for the owner.
- **Accepted records are immutable; change means supersede.** AWS says an accepted ADR "becomes immutable", Microsoft calls the log "append-only", and Fowler says an accepted record "should never be reopened or changed" (`accepted-records-immutable`). A new verdict, **supersede-never-edit-accepted-records**, holds at SHOULD.
- **Decision supersession.** A decision that changes an earlier one is a new record. The new record marks the old one superseded and the two are linked. Microsoft says "link the two together". Nygard marks the old record with "a reference to its replacement". GDS says the old ADR "must be clearly marked" (`supersede-and-link`). Recording decisions stops teams reopening settled questions and contradicting earlier intent without knowing it (`records-prevent-relitigation`). **acknowledge-contradicted-decisions** is supported: SHOULD in Microsoft's and AWS's wording, MUST in GDS's and PEP 1's. A new verdict, **supersession-links-both-ways**, also holds at SHOULD, with MUST available: the old record points to its replacement and the new record names what it replaces (`supersede-and-link`, `supersession-header-fields`, `gds-way-supersede-must`).
- **Status lifecycle.** Each record carries a status: proposed, accepted or superseded, and MADR adds rejected and deprecated (`status-lifecycle`). New verdict **record-status-lifecycle**, SHOULD.
- **Date.** Google Cloud's timestamp, MADR's `date` and AWS's approval timestamp all carry the date (`record-decision-date`). New verdict **record-decision-date**, SHOULD.
- **One decision, kept short.** Nygard puts one decision in each record, Fowler keeps a record to a couple of pages, and Henderson's collection says one decision per ADR (`one-decision-short-record`). New verdict **one-decision-per-short-record**, SHOULD.
- **Significant decisions only.** Microsoft includes only structural, quality-affecting or hard-to-reverse choices. AWS records every architecturally significant decision. Spotify leaves each team to define significant impact (`record-significant-decisions`). New verdict **record-significant-decisions-only**, SHOULD.
- **Findable and linked to the work.** Microsoft stores the log "openly". GDS asks teams to make ADRs discoverable. GOV.UK says to make approved records accessible to stakeholders (`store-openly-findable`). Records for one codebase live in its version control, and broader decisions go in a central repository or wiki (`store-with-the-code`). Records link to the issues that carry them out and to the code review that enforces them (`link-decisions-to-work`). GitHub links a pull request to its issues by keyword (`github-issue-pr-linking`). **decisions-findable-and-linked** is supported at SHOULD.
- **Rejected proposals are kept.** AWS records the reason for a rejection and PEP 1 keeps rejected PEPs (`record-rejected-decisions`). New verdict **record-rejected-proposals**, SHOULD.
- **Confidence level.** Microsoft and Fowler record how confident the decision was and what would trigger revisiting it (`record-confidence-level`). New verdict **record-confidence-level**, MAY.
- **Backfill.** Microsoft generates records retroactively for existing systems when the data exists, and Spotify points to undocumented implicit decisions (`backfill-undocumented`). New verdict **backfill-undocumented-decisions**, MAY.

## Known solutions

- **Nygard's template** has Title, Status, Context, Decision and Consequences. Records are numbered files in the repository and numbers are never reused. It has no alternatives or decider section, and adr-tools ships the same shape (`nygard-template`).
- **MADR** adds Decision Drivers, Considered Options with pros and cons, and optional decision-makers, consulted and informed metadata. Its minimal variant still keeps Considered Options (`madr-template`).
- **Y-statements** put context, requirement, the chosen option, the rejected options and the accepted downside in one sentence (`y-statement`).
- **adr-tools** creates numbered records. Its `-s` flag writes a new record that supersedes an old one and updates the old record's status, which gives supersession links in both directions (`adr-tools-supersede-command`).
- **log4brains** publishes repository ADRs as a searchable static site with a timeline. It uses MADR by default and needs no sequential numbers, which avoids merge conflicts (`log4brains-knowledge-base`).
- **Confluence** offers a Decisions blueprint whose index is a decision log, and a DACI template that lists at least three options (`confluence-decision-log`).
- **Standards processes** carry supersession as header fields in both directions: PEP Superseded-By and Replaces, RFC Obsoletes and Updates (`supersession-header-fields`). Rust RFCs and PEPs require sections for alternatives and rejected ideas (`proposal-processes-record-alternatives`).
- **Adoption.** One mining study of GitHub repositories found ADR adoption low but growing. About half of the adopting repositories hold one to five records, and Nygard's template is the most used. This is a single study, read as its abstract, so it is context and not support for any rule (`adr-adoption-study`).

## Platform guidelines

- **AWS, Microsoft and Google Cloud** agree that a changed decision keeps the earlier one visible. AWS and Microsoft use a new superseding record, and Google Cloud records the previous decision and why it changed (`cloud-providers-agree-on-supersession`). Their guidance on contents, immutability, status and storage is cited under Best practices.
- **UK government.** The GDS Way requires a superseded record to be marked directly under its heading and linked to its replacement (`gds-way-supersede-must`). The GOV.UK ADR framework sets the record's fields and assigns decision-makers by decision level. It is mandated only for cross-government decisions and recommended at every level (`govuk-adr-framework`).
- **GitHub** links pull requests to issues by keyword, across repositories (`github-issue-pr-linking`). **Jira** has configurable link types and no default type for supersession (`jira-link-types-no-supersedes`). **Linear** relates issues as related, blocking or blocked (`linear-issue-relations`).
- **Scrum Guide** ties decisions to artifact transparency and defines no decision record (`scrum-guide-no-decision-record`). The **Kanban Guide** mentions decisions only as informed by flow metrics (`kanban-guide-no-decision-record`).
- **PMBOK, ISO 21502, ISO 31000, PRINCE2, SAFe and LeSS.** Their own texts were not reachable, so their positions are not established (see limitations).
- **Apple, Android and Windows** have no bearing on this topic.

## The expert's view

- **Why records matter.** Without recorded motivation, later teams either accept a decision blindly or reverse it blindly (`nygard-motivation-is-lost`). Writing the record also clarifies a group's thinking, and brevity matters most (`fowler-writing-clarifies`).
- **Name the decider and judge every option.** Named decision-makers are accountable for enforcing the decision, and the options that lost are evaluated too (`zimmermann-identify-decision-makers`).
- **What people get wrong.** Common mistakes are justifying a decision with pros only and recording no consequences, or only harmless ones (`zimmermann-anti-patterns`). Another is inventing a dummy alternative to make the favourite shine, or recording only one option. Consider at least two real options (`zimmermann-dummy-alternative-and-sprint`), which supports the new verdict **no-dummy-alternatives** at SHOULD. A record should not grow into multi-page architecture documentation (`zimmermann-mega-adr`). Pick one template, keep to it, and share each record once it is reviewed (`zimmermann-socialize-and-one-template`).
- **Dissent on immutability.** GDS lets a clarification edit a record before it is implemented, and Henderson's teams amend records with dated notes (`clarifications-may-amend`). That is against Microsoft, AWS and Fowler, so it is left as an open question.
- **Practice.** Decisions are reached through RFCs or meetings, the ADR records the outcome, and peer review surfaces undocumented decisions (`spotify-rfc-then-adr`). Thoughtworks put lightweight ADRs in its Adopt ring and prefers keeping them in source control (`thoughtworks-adopt`). An imperfect log that is accurate and current beats having none (`inconsistent-log-better-than-none`).
