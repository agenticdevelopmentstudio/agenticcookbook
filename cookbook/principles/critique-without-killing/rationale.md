
- `should-target-the-idea-not-the-person`: Nemeth & Rogers (1996): devil's advocate groups generated more alternatives and made better
  decisions than groups without — but ONLY when the challenge targeted the idea, not the person
- `must-propose-alternative-after-every-objection`: Tom Kelley (IDEO, 2005): unstructured devil's advocacy "kills creativity" by providing social
  cover for killing ideas without accountability; the constructive version requires proposing an
  alternative direction after every objection
- `should-critique-with-questions-not-verdicts`: Schulze-Bentrop et al. (2025, arXiv 2503.14263): AI counterarguments framed as open questions
  significantly enhanced process and outcome satisfaction, especially for lower-power participants
