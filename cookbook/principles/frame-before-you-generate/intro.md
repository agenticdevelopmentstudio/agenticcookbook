
# Frame Before You Generate

Find the real problem before generating solutions. The stated problem is rarely the real one;
investing in problem reframing before ideation begins unlocks a richer solution space and prevents
wasted generative effort on the wrong goal.

- Spend real time on problem definition: the Double Diamond (Design Council, 2005) devotes its
  entire first diamond to finding the right problem before the second diamond seeks solutions
- Use "How Might We" framing (IDEO; Min Basadur, 1990s) to convert closed problem statements into
  generative invitations: "How *Might* We [verb] [goal]?" assumes solvability, invites speculation
  without commitment, and creates shared ownership
- Apply Five Whys (Toyoda/Ohno, Toyota Production System, 1930s) to drill past stated symptoms to
  root causes; a stated goal is often a symptom, not the underlying need
- Use first-principles thinking to strip inherited assumptions and rebuild the problem from
  fundamental truths (Stanford d.school Empathize→Define, 2010; Aristotle; Musk/SpaceX)
- A good reframe often reveals that the originally-stated goal was solving the wrong level of problem
