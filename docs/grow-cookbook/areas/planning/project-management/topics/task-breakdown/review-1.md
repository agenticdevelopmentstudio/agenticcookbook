## Findings

- `rules.md` **link-tasks-to-milestones**, plus its echoes in other parts (source fidelity, and owner-list open question 6). The research supports this rule only "at SHOULD in projects that use milestones" (research.md, Known solutions: "The sources treat milestones as optional grouping"). The cited `github-milestones-group-issues` says "You *can* use milestones". The rule drops that scope and applies to every task, which outruns its claims and answers the owner's open question ("whether every task must link to a milestone, or only tasks in a project that uses milestones"). Every instance: `rules.md` link-tasks-to-milestones ("Each task SHOULD be linked…"); `a-build-must-establish.md` ("every task is linked to the milestone it serves"); `a-complete-review-must-establish.md` ("Every task is linked to the milestone it serves."); `what-a-reviewer-looks-for.md` ("A task not linked to the milestone it serves."); `intro.md` ("link each task to the milestone it serves"); `breaking-work-into-tasks.md` step 5 ("link each task to the milestone or Sprint Goal it serves"). Fix: scope the rule the way the research does ("Where a project uses milestones, each task SHOULD be linked to the milestone it serves…"), and give the same scope to the bar, review-bar, review-focus, intro and how-to lines.
- `rules.md` **blocker-links-the-blocking-item**, plus its echoes (source fidelity, and owner-list open question 5). The rule, and the parts that repeat it, require every blocked task to link to a blocking *item*. One of its two cited claims, `waiting-for-names-whom`, is the research's evidence that a blocker "can be a person or outside thing, not only a tracked item" (research.md, Known solutions). So the rule does not follow from that claim, and it answers the owner's open question ("whether a blocker must name a tracked item … or may name an outside cause in text"). Every instance: `rules.md` blocker-links-the-blocking-item; `a-build-must-establish.md` ("every blocked task has a \"blocked by\" link to what it waits on"); `a-complete-review-must-establish.md` ("linked to its specific blocker"); `what-a-reviewer-looks-for.md` ("with no \"blocked by\" link"); `handling-a-blocked-task.md` step 2 ("Link the blocked task to the item it waits on"); `intro.md` ("record each blocked task's blocker as a link"). Fix: scope the rule to blockers that are tracked items ("Where a blocked task waits on a tracked item, it SHOULD record it as a \"blocked by\" link…"), say nothing about outside causes, and give the same scope to the bar, review-bar, review-focus, how-to and intro lines.
- `breaking-work-into-tasks.md` step 6 and `examples.md` contradict the owner-listed MUST **task-has-assignee-and-priority** ("Each todo MUST pass this check: Every todo has an assignee and priority"), and they answer owner-list open question 1. Every instance: step 6, "Leave tasks unassigned until work on them begins", tells the reader to break the MUST and settles the question in Cohn's favour. The worked task in `examples.md` (Title / Action / Done when / Expect / Milestone) has no assignee and no priority, so the guideline's own good example fails its MUST. No how-to step sets a priority or an assignee on a todo. Fix: reword step 6 so it no longer tells the reader to leave tasks unassigned (for example, "Whoever starts a task becomes its one assignee"). Add `Assignee:` and `Priority:` lines to the worked example. Add a how-to step that sets each todo's assignee and priority, so the informative parts satisfy the rule they sit beside and leave the open question to the owner.
- The review and build parts state more than their rules (behavioral-requirements must-be-independently-testable: the bars must test what the rule says). Every instance: **breakdown-covers-all-scope** says only that the breakdown includes all the scope's work, with children adding up to their parent. It rightly leaves out "nothing outside scope", because the second check found that `wbs-hundred-percent-rule`'s quote does not state it. Yet `a-build-must-establish.md` ("and hold nothing outside it"), `a-complete-review-must-establish.md` ("and nothing outside it"), `what-a-reviewer-looks-for.md` ("or holds work outside it") and `breaking-work-into-tasks.md` step 1 ("and leave out anything outside it") all add it. **identify-blocked-tasks-and-blockers** marks a task as blocked only "where the tracker allows". `a-complete-review-must-establish.md` ("marked as blocked") and `what-a-reviewer-looks-for.md` ("A blocked task not marked as blocked") drop that condition. Fix: remove the out-of-scope clause from the four lines, and add "where the tracker allows" to the two blocked-marking lines.
- `rules.md`: compound rules (behavioral-requirements must-not-bundle-behaviors-into-one-requirement and must-be-independently-testable). Several rules not on the owner list each bundle behaviours that can pass or fail separately, so the build bar cannot pass or fail a part. Every instance: **break-down-assign-and-prioritize-tasks** (break larger work into tasks; assign responsibility to a person or team; order the tasks, and the ordering also duplicates backlog-is-kept-ordered); **tasks-specific-and-actionable** (specific enough for everyone to understand; relevant to its story; a clear, defined outcome; a short title that states the task); **task-text-names-action-and-done-test** (names a physical, visible action; carries a done test the team agrees on); **identify-blocked-tasks-and-blockers** (identify each blocked task and its blocker; mark it blocked where the tracker allows; act to unblock it in a regular review of active work); **work-package-within-eighty-hours** (at most about 80 hours of effort; no longer than one reporting period). Fix: keep each prior name (break-down-assign-and-prioritize-tasks, tasks-specific-and-actionable, identify-blocked-tasks-and-blockers) on one behaviour, and split each other behaviour into its own named rule with its own citation and its own build-bar line. Where a part is already covered by another rule (ordering by backlog-is-kept-ordered), drop it instead of splitting it. Split the new rules task-text-names-action-and-done-test and work-package-within-eighty-hours the same way.
- `rules.md`: unanchored relative terms (behavioral-requirements should-not-use-unanchored-relative-terms). Every instance: **split-large-estimates**, "a large estimate", has no threshold, although task-sized-hours-to-a-few-days supplies one (a week or more). **done-test-is-pass-fail**, "neither too vague nor too detailed": "too detailed" has no threshold, and the pass/fail clause already rules out "too vague". **tasks-specific-and-actionable**, "a short title", has no length or test. Fix: anchor "large" to the task-size rule (for example, "an estimate of a week or more, or none"), drop "nor too detailed" or give it a test, and replace "short" with a test (the title "states directly what the task is" already carries the requirement, so "short" can go).
- `rationale.md`, paragraph 4 ("The vertical-slice, 100%-rule and GTD claims were each found by the second check to support their claim only in part…"). The second check refuted five claims, not three. The paragraph leaves out `decompose-to-one-day-or-less` (cited by break-down-assign-and-prioritize-tasks and task-sized-hours-to-a-few-days) and `trackers-record-blocked-by-links` (cited by identify-blocked-tasks-and-blockers and blocker-links-the-blocking-item). That makes it look as if those rules rest on fully confirmed claims. Fix: name all five refuted claims, and the rules that cite them, in that paragraph.
